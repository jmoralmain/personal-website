What the board says about drafting here

  • Running backs were the value play. RBs taken in rounds 1–5 beat their slot by 70 points on average. Wide receivers there came in 39 under, and tight ends 58 under.
  • Early tight ends were a trap. Of the five taken in the first five rounds, Sam LaPorta and Dalton Kincaid have almost nothing above replacement since 2024.

How this was measured. Data from the Sleeper public API, pulled 19 November 2026: every completed trade in the 2024, 2025 and 2026 leagues, weekly lineups, and every player's weekly stats scored with the league's own settings (superflex, full PPR, unchanged across seasons). Weeks counted: 2024 and 2025 weeks 1–17, 2026 weeks 1–10.

What a team received is worth the points above replacement its new players scored in weeks they were in its starting lineup, from the trade's week until they left the roster. Replacement is that week's 24th-best QB, 30th RB, 42nd WR and 14th TE among players who played, the same levels the draft page uses. A week below replacement counts as zero. A traded pick is credited to the team that used it, for the player it drafted, from that draft on. A pick that was traded again before the draft is worth nothing to the team that passed it on; its value shows up in the later trade. A pick for 2027 or later counts at its expected value from the Draft picks tab: the points above replacement a rookie taken there typically produces over his first season and a half, using the 2027 order projected from the standings and a round average for 2028 and 2029. Net value is what a team received minus the value of what it sent away.

Caveats. Only starts count, so a player held on the bench as insurance earns nothing. The 37 picks for 2027 and later are worth 1,244 at expected value, which is a projection, not production; the 2027 slots will move with the standings. The 2026 rookies have had ten weeks. 2026 is through week 10. A trade is "too close" when the margin is under 15 points and "too early" when most of its assets are 2026 rookies or future picks. One trade also moved $30 of FAAB, which is not valued.

With only 36 picks it is a rough expectation, so small gaps between picks don't mean much. A team's grade is the sum over the picks it made, whoever owned the slot first.

"Starter hits" counts picks with a starter-grade season (top 24 QB, top 24 RB, top 36 WR, top 12 TE). "Starts" counts weeks the drafting team put the rookie in its lineup (2025 weeks 1–17, 2026 weeks 1–10). "Still here" counts rookies on the drafting team's roster today. Slot is where the team's own first-round pick fell, with its 2024 record.

Did teams use their rookies?

Less than expected. 16 of the 36 rookies have never started for the team that drafted them. 26 are still on the drafting roster, 6 play for someone else and 4 are free agents. The six who moved have produced 221 points above replacement this season, close to the 255 from the 26 who stayed.

  • The breakouts were sold before they broke out. Matthew Golden (WR88 as a rookie, WR6 now) left GAVINKIRK before this season and now plays for Perriconejp. Bhayshul Tuten (RB55, then RB15) was traded by mynameisuser for Rashee Rice and never started for it. Luther Burden (WR49, then WR15) never started for brookseleven, which sold him to mynameisuser in 2025 for a 2026 1st.
  • Henderson went the other way. Bengraz03 traded TreVeyon Henderson to mynameisuser in week 3 of his rookie year. He finished RB21 in 2025 and is RB62 this year.
  • The heaviest users were the first-round picks that hit. Jeanty (26 starts), McMillan (25), Warren (25) and Judkins (20) carried most of the 227 starts drafting teams gave their rookies.

Positional trends

  • Running backs went first and mostly paid. Half of round 1 was backs, and 6 of the 12 backs taken have had a starter-grade season.
  • Receivers are arriving in year two. 10 of 14 have nothing above replacement yet. As a group they scored 128 points above replacement in all of 2025 and 199 in ten weeks of 2026, led by Golden and Burden.
  • Tight ends were a waste of picks so far. Five went, and only Tyler Warren has a season above replacement. The group has 2 points above replacement this year.
  • Quarterbacks were the best value per pick. Three of the five (Cam Ward, Jaxson Dart, Tyler Shough) have had a starter-grade season, and the group beat expectation by 6 points on average.
  • The third round was close to empty. Round 1 picks averaged 58 points above replacement, round 2 picks 16 and round 3 picks 4. Shough is the only third-rounder above zero.

Still open questions

A season and a half is short for rookies, and receivers and tight ends often take until year three. These are the picks where today's number could look very different by 2027.

  • Travis Hunter (#4). His value depends on how much offense Jacksonville gives a player who also plays corner. The talent that made him a #4 pick hasn't shown up in fantasy points yet.
  • Omarion Hampton (#2) and Kaleb Johnson (#11). Both are young backs with time left. Hampton has moved from RB33 to RB28. Johnson has 27 points this year.
  • Colston Loveland (#15). He finished exactly at the TE replacement line as a rookie (TE14) and is TE16 now. Tight ends are the slowest to develop, so his −25 is the softest number on the board.
  • The 2025 hits that faded. TreVeyon Henderson (RB21 to RB62), RJ Harvey (RB18 to RB34), Emeka Egbuka (WR20 to WR45) and Jaxson Dart (QB16 to QB39) all had starter-grade rookie years and haven't repeated them.
  • Jayden Higgins (#14). WR51 as a rookie and no games played in 2026.
  • Cam Ward (#7). QB22 and QB21: a starter in superflex, but barely above replacement. Whether he grows into more decides mynameisuser's grade.

How this was measured. Data from the Sleeper public API, pulled 18 November 2026: the 2026 rookie draft (3 rounds, 12 teams, linear order, 36 picks), every player's weekly NFL stats for 2026 weeks 1–10, scored with the league's settings (superflex, full PPR). Points above replacement (PAR) is a player's 2026 total minus that of the 24th QB (105.0), 30th RB (75.9), 42nd WR (81.3) or 14th TE (84.3) through week 10, floored at 0. These are the same levels the startup page uses.

Value over slot compares a pick with the mean of the seven picks centered on it (three on each side, fewer at the ends of the draft). It is shown two ways: on raw 2026 points (points over slot, used for grades and the default board) and on PAR. A weekly starter finish is a top-24 QB, top-24 RB, top-36 WR or top-12 TE week by points. Games played uses Sleeper's games-played flag, not its active flag, which is also set for players on IR. Snap share comes from Sleeper's offensive snap counts.

Caveats: ten games is a small sample, and a rookie's value is mostly in future seasons this page can't see. A pick's value follows the player wherever he went, so it measures the pick, not what the team did with him later. Starts and lineup data come from the league's weekly matchups. Depth chart and injury status are Sleeper's values on 18 November.
