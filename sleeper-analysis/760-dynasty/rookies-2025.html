The grades

Each pick is worth its points above replacement: how much the player outscored a freely available player at his position, season by season, with a season below replacement counted as zero. Value over slot subtracts what a pick at that spot was expected to return: a smooth curve that falls with each pick, fitted to this draft's own 36 picks (105.6 points above replacement at #1, 36.7 at #10, 17.5 at #19, 1.9 at #32). With only 36 picks it is a rough expectation, so small gaps between picks don't mean much. A team's grade is the sum over the picks it made, whoever owned the slot first.

"Starter hits" counts picks with a starter-grade season (top 24 QB, top 24 RB, top 36 WR, top 12 TE). "Starts" counts weeks the drafting team put the rookie in its lineup (2025 weeks 1–17, 2026 weeks 1–10). "Still here" counts rookies on the drafting team's roster today. Slot is where the team's own first-round pick fell, with its 2024 record.

Traded picks

14 of the 36 picks were made by a team other than the one that owned the slot. Those picks came in 4 points under expectation on average, and the picks teams kept came in 2 over. Buying a 2025 pick mostly paid off only for GAVINKIRK, masonmay and mynameisuser.

  • Perriconejp's Puka Nacua trade bought three picks that returned nothing. masonmay's 1st, 2nd and 3rd (#11, #23, #35) became Kaleb Johnson, Jack Bech and Dylan Sampson: no points above replacement and no starts, 46 under expectation combined. Perriconejp hasn't needed them. It is 9-1.
  • GAVINKIRK sold well and bought well. It traded Drake Maye for #10 plus ryanoleson5's 2026 1st, turned #10 into Golden, then sold its own #4 on draft day.
  • TannerMenning traded up for the worst result. The Hunter move cost DK Metcalf and a 1st. Its trade of #12 and #25 to masonmay for Davante Adams and J.K. Dobbins gave away Emeka Egbuka, a WR20 rookie.
  • mynameisuser's Cooper Kupp sale worked. djklotzshotz's 1st became Quinshon Judkins (#9, +23), a starter-grade back in both seasons with 20 starts. On draft day it swapped #13 for James Conner and #27; #13 became Cam Skattebo for KDEMPSTER15 and #27 was cut.

Did teams use their rookies?

Less than you'd expect. 16 of the 36 rookies have never started for the team that drafted them. 26 are still on the drafting roster, 6 play for someone else and 4 are free agents. The six who moved have produced 221 points above replacement this season, close to the 255 from the 26 who stayed.

  • The breakouts were sold before they broke out. Matthew Golden (WR88 as a rookie, WR6 now) left GAVINKIRK before this season and now plays for Perriconejp. Bhayshul Tuten (RB55, then RB15) was traded by mynameisuser for Rashee Rice and never started for it. Luther Burden (WR49, then WR15) never started for brookseleven, which sold him to mynameisuser in 2025 for a 2026 1st.
  • Henderson went the other way. Bengraz03 traded TreVeyon Henderson to mynameisuser in week 3 of his rookie year. He finished RB21 in 2025 and is RB62 this year.
  • The heaviest users were the first-round picks that hit. Jeanty (26 starts), McMillan (25), Warren (25) and Judkins (20) carried most of the 227 starts drafting teams gave their rookies.

Positional trends

  • Running backs went first and mostly paid. Half of round 1 was backs, and 6 of the 12 backs taken have had a starter-grade season.
  • Receivers are arriving in year two. 10 of 14 have nothing above replacement yet. As a group they scored 128 points above replacement in all of 2025 and 199 in ten weeks of 2026, led by Golden and Burden.
  • Tight ends were a waste of picks so far. Five went, and only Tyler Warren has a season above replacement. The group has 2 points above replacement this year.
  • Quarterbacks were the best value per pick. Three of the five (Cam Ward, Jaxson Dart, Tyler Shough) have had a starter-grade season, and the group beat expectation by 6 points on average.
  • The third round was close to empty. Round 1 picks averaged 58 points above replacement, round 2 picks 16 and round 3 picks 4. Shough is the only third-rounder above zero.

Still open questions

A season and a half is short for rookies, and receivers and tight ends often take until year three. These are the picks where today's number could look very different by 2027.

  • Travis Hunter (#4). His value depends on how much offense Jacksonville gives a player who also plays corner. The talent that made him a #4 pick hasn't shown up in fantasy points yet.
  • Omarion Hampton (#2) and Kaleb Johnson (#11). Both are young backs with time left. Hampton has moved from RB33 to RB28. Johnson has 27 points this year.
  • Colston Loveland (#15). He finished exactly at the TE replacement line as a rookie (TE14) and is TE16 now. Tight ends are the slowest to develop, so his −25 is the softest number on the board.
  • The 2025 hits that faded. TreVeyon Henderson (RB21 to RB62), RJ Harvey (RB18 to RB34), Emeka Egbuka (WR20 to WR45) and Jaxson Dart (QB16 to QB39) all had starter-grade rookie years and haven't repeated them.
  • Jayden Higgins (#14). WR51 as a rookie and no games played in 2026.
  • Cam Ward (#7). QB22 and QB21: a starter in superflex, but barely above replacement. Whether he grows into more decides mynameisuser's grade.

Replacement level is that season's points total of the 24th QB, 30th RB, 42nd WR and 14th TE, the same line as the startup draft page. Value over slot compares a pick with a curve of expected value by pick number (least squares on the log of the pick, floored at zero), fitted to this draft's own 36 picks. That makes it a rough expectation, and gaps of a few points between picks are noise. It counts every point a player scored, whoever owned him, so it measures the pick and not what the team did with the player later. Games played counts weeks with a recorded game, since Sleeper also marks inactive players as active. Rookie values this early are provisional: nothing from 2027 on is counted, and a slow start for a receiver or tight end is common.
